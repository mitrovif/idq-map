These are drafts for review, not enumerator text.

The names are UCDP’s, not a respondent’s. UCDP writes “JAS” where a Nigerian respondent says Boko Haram. A handful of the best-known are translated here; the rest need someone in the country.

Naming actors risks anchoring. Someone displaced by a group not named may conclude the option does not cover them — the opposite of what an example is for. Where the data allowed, a category phrase was preferred to a name, and each example is tagged with which it is.

Identity groups are never named. UCDP codes communal violence as, for example, “Christians (Nigeria) – Muslims (Nigeria)”. Correct as conflict coding, unusable in an instrument a government enumerator reads aloud. Those become “communal or intercommunal violence”.

Options 3 and 7 stay generic almost everywhere because no event source records discrimination, persecution or development-induced displacement. That is a finding about the data, not about those countries.

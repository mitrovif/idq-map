# What the databases cannot see

## Qualitative evidence for response codes 3, 4 and 7, across the twenty largest displacement contexts

Research note, 3 August 2026. Companion to the causing-events map and the crosswalk explorer.

Countries covered, ranked by displaced population originating there (IDPs plus refugees produced):
Sudan, Syria, Ukraine, Colombia, DR Congo, Afghanistan, Myanmar, Yemen, Somalia, Nigeria,
South Sudan, Ethiopia, Burkina Faso, Haiti, Venezuela, Iraq, Central African Republic,
Cameroon, Mali, Mozambique. Together roughly 85% of the world's displaced people.

Every factual claim below is attributed to a named source. Where the search found nothing,
that is stated rather than filled in.

---

## The headline finding: code 7 is not rare, it is uncounted

The database analysis returned **code 7 (man-made events) as UNEVIDENCED in all 182
countries**. Even after reassigning wildfire, dam-release floods and sinkholes to it, the
entire global total was 695,595 people — 99.8% of it wildfire.

That conclusion does not survive contact with the qualitative record. In the twenty
countries researched:

| Country | Documented development-induced or eviction displacement | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Somalia | **1.5 million** people affected by forced eviction, 2018–2024; 100,907 evicted in Mogadishu in 2023 alone; 87% of Mogadishu's displaced lack tenure | [NRC](https://www.nrc.no/resources/reports/community-driven-consultations-on-hlp-needs-among-minority-marginalised-displaced-communities-in-mogadishu) |

**Somalia alone documents more code 7 displacement than the entire global IDMC figure.**

This is the sharpest possible demonstration of the paper's core argument. The instrument's
code 7 is not a speculative category kept for completeness. It describes a mass phenomenon
that global displacement statistics do not count because no agency holds the mandate to
count it. The response option is doing work the data system cannot.

**Revision to the earlier conclusion:** code 7 should be reported as *uncounted*, never as
*rare*, and the qualitative record should be cited alongside the null database result.
Reporting the zero without this context would be actively misleading.

## Code 3: persecution is usually the operative reason, recorded as conflict

The crosswalk found that persecution-driven displacement is dissolved into code 1 by IDMC's
classification. The qualitative record shows how completely, and how often the respondent's
own account is explicitly about identity rather than fighting.

- **El Geneina, Sudan.** RSF and allied militias screened people at checkpoints by tribe.
  A survivor: *"If you answered that you are Massalit, they would kill you immediately."*
  Over 500,000 crossed into Chad. HRW characterises it as ethnic cleansing.
  ([HRW](https://www.hrw.org/report/2024/05/09/the-massalit-will-not-come-home/ethnic-cleansing-and-crimes-against-humanity-in))
- **Central Mali.** A witness to HRW: *"We Fulani all fled because we know that the soldiers
  will go after us."* Not caught in fighting — anticipating targeted state violence.
  ([HRW](https://www.hrw.org/news/2025/07/22/mali-army-wagner-group-disappear-execute-fulani-civilians))
- **Yaloké, CAR.** A town of ~30,000 Muslims and eight mosques had fewer than 500 Muslims
  and one mosque left by February 2014, after anti-balaka gave *"the Peuhl and any Muslim of
  Chadian descent 24 hours to leave the city or face attack."* Nobody fled a battle.
  ([HRW](https://www.hrw.org/news/2014/02/12/central-african-republic-muslims-forced-flee))
- **Western Tigray, Ethiopia.** Ultimatums of 24–72 hours, identity documents seized before
  people were allowed to cross the Tekeze, homes marked *"Amhara house"*, bans on speaking
  Tigrinya. 723,000 IDPs registered from Western Tigray by June 2021; 740,000 still
  displaced in April 2026.
  ([HRW/Amnesty](https://www.hrw.org/report/2022/04/06/we-will-erase-you-land/crimes-against-humanity-and-ethnic-cleansing-ethiopias))
- **Juba, South Sudan, December 2013.** Government forces conducted house-to-house searches
  screening for ethnicity; over 25,000 Nuer fled to UN bases.
  ([HRW](https://www.hrw.org/news/2014/01/16/south-sudan-ethnic-targeting-widespread-killings))
- **Solenzo, Burkina Faso, March 2025.** VDP militia filmed calling for Fulani
  *"extermination"*; a survivor: *"they wanted to exterminate us all."*
  ([HRW](https://www.hrw.org/news/2025/05/12/burkina-faso-army-directs-ethnic-massacres))

In every one of these, IDMC would record conflict displacement and ACLED would record a
violence event. Neither records why the person was selected.

### Code 3's example list may not be recognisable to the people it describes

Three findings bear directly on question wording:

1. **Cameroon — language group.** Anglophone Cameroonians are persecuted for a linguistic
   and educational identity. HRW documents detainees abused as *"Anglo fools"* and operations
   confined to the two Anglophone regions. This falls under "other group membership", but a
   respondent scanning *"ethnic group, nationality, religion, political beliefs, sexual
   orientation"* may not see themselves. **Consider naming language group explicitly.**
   ([HRW](https://www.hrw.org/report/2018/07/19/these-killings-can-be-stopped/abuses-government-and-separatist-groups-cameroons))
2. **Somalia — clan and caste.** The code 3 signal is overwhelmingly about clan and
   minority-caste status (Rahanweyn, Somali Bantu), not the categories a European drafter
   would expect. Police told displaced people: *"You are from Bay and Bakool regions. You
   are al-Shabaab's people... you are cursed people."*
   ([HRW](https://www.hrw.org/report/2013/03/28/hostages-gatekeepers/abuses-against-internally-displaced-mogadishu-somalia))
3. **Perceived, not actual, identity drives targeting.** CAR Muslims holding CAR citizenship
   were targeted as "foreigners". In Mozambique, insurgents *"killed only civilians who
   presented government-issued or Frelimo identification cards"* — the trigger was a document,
   not an identity. A question asking about the respondent's *own* group membership will miss
   displacement caused by an attacker's misattribution.
   ([Crisis Group](https://crisisgroup.org/sites/default/files/303-mozambique-cabo-delgado%20(1).pdf))

### Where SOGI-based displacement was and was not found

**Colombia is the only country of the twenty with a formal judicial finding.** The Special
Jurisdiction for Peace accepted jurisdiction in Macro Case 005 over gender-based persecution
on grounds of sexual orientation and gender identity as a potential crime against humanity,
covering acts including forced displacement by FARC-EP, paramilitaries **and state security
forces**.
([EJIL:Talk!](https://www.ejiltalk.org/gender-based-persecution-as-a-crime-against-humanity-a-milestone-for-lgbti-rights-before-the-colombian-special-jurisdiction-for-peace/))

Afghanistan has documented flight — HRW and Outright interviewed 60 LGBT Afghans, many of
whom had left their homes after gang rape, mob violence and Taliban threats
([HRW](https://www.hrw.org/report/2022/01/26/even-if-you-go-skies-well-find-you/lgbt-people-afghanistan-after-taliban-takeover)).
Elsewhere — Nigeria, Cameroon, Iraq, Mali, Venezuela — **abuse is documented but displacement
is not**. Criminalisation is documented (Iraq 2024, Mali 2024); flight is not.

That asymmetry is itself the argument for keeping the SOGI clause and reading it aloud. The
absence of documentation is what one would expect for a form of persecution nobody
systematically records — which is precisely the case the survey is placed to correct.

## Code 4: "authorities" is the weakest word in the instrument

Four of the five research streams independently flagged the same problem. In the territories
people flee *from*, the entity that detains, tortures and confiscates is frequently a de
facto authority rather than a recognised government:

- **M23** in eastern DRC — forcibly recruiting and detaining thousands, Rumangabo and Tshanzu
  camps, *"hundreds, perhaps more"* dead
  ([HRW](https://www.hrw.org/news/2026/06/10/dr-congo-rwanda-m23-forcibly-recruit-detain-thousands))
- **The RSF** in Darfur — the UN Fact-Finding Mission documents mass arbitrary detention by
  both SAF and RSF; "authorities" means different actors in Port Sudan and Nyala
  ([OHCHR](https://www.ohchr.org/en/press-releases/2026/06/sudan-arbitrary-detention-torture-and-enforced-disappearances-fueling))
- **The Houthis** in Yemen — 69 UN employees in detention as of December 2025
  ([HRW](https://www.hrw.org/world-report/2026/country-chapters/yemen))
- **The Arakan Army** in Rakhine
  ([HRW](https://www.hrw.org/news/2025/07/28/myanmar-arakan-army-oppresses-rohingya-muslims))
- **Wagner / Africa Corps** operating jointly with state forces in CAR and Mali — UN experts
  noted victims in CAR cannot obtain redress *because the perpetrators operate jointly with
  state forces*, and respondents may not know whether the men who took their relatives were
  "authorities" at all
  ([OHCHR](https://reliefweb.int/report/central-african-republic/car-russian-wagner-group-harassing-and-intimidating-civilians-un))

**Recommendation.** Define "authorities" in enumerator guidance as *whoever exercised
governmental functions in the place you were living at the time*, and test respondent
interpretation of the term explicitly in cognitive interviews. Read narrowly as "the
government", a large share of genuine code 4 displacement will be misclassified into codes
1 or 2.

### A structural gap in the evidence, which is itself an argument for the survey

Named sources document detention, torture and disappearance thoroughly — UNAMA logged 800+
instances in Afghanistan; Amnesty documented 14,500 arrested in Myanmar; OHCHR found Venezuelan
state conduct amounts to crimes against humanity — but **almost never quantify onward flight**.

The exceptions are informative: HRW explicitly documents Yemen's Baha'is *"forced into exile"*,
a South Sudanese journalist helped across the border after nine months of detention and
waterboarding, and 5,800 people fleeing Apure into Colombia during Venezuela's Operation
Bolivarian Shield.

Nobody counts how many people leave because of state repression. A household survey with a
code 4 response option would produce the first such estimate. **This is worth saying
explicitly in the paper** — it turns a weakness of the crosswalk into a justification for
the instrument.

## Five design implications

**1. Multiple response is not a convenience, it is a correctness requirement.**
Every research stream reached this independently. The displacement events that matter most
are multiply caused: El Geneina is codes 1+2+3; occupied Mariupol is 1+4+7; Damascus's
Basateen al-Razi is 4+7; Kabul's demolitions are 3+7; Montepuez is 4+7. If a single response
is forced, codes 3, 4 and 7 will be absorbed into codes 1 and 2 — **reproducing exactly the
bias in IDMC, ACLED and UCDP that the question exists to correct.** The desk review already
recommends multi-code; this is the evidentiary case for it.

**2. Respondents narrate acts, not reasons — which structurally favours code 4 over code 3.**
In Iraq and Venezuela the *reason* is group membership (code 3) but the *act* is state abuse
(code 4). "They took my son" is what a respondent says. Cognitive testing should establish
whether respondents can hold both, or whether code 4 crowds out code 3.

**3. Code 1's word "war" will fail in Haiti, and probably elsewhere.**
The Geneva Academy's War Watch concludes the Viv Ansanm situation legally *is* a
non-international armed conflict; the ICRC publicly uses "armed violence" instead. The word
used in Haitian Creole is *bandi* — bandits. A respondent primed by "war" will reject code 1
even where an analyst would apply it, pushing mass conflict displacement into codes 2 or 5.
The instrument's treatment of criminal-armed-group violence needs explicit testing.
([Lieber Institute](https://lieber.westpoint.edu/haitis-gang-violence-armed-conflict-threshold-ihl-analysis/))

**4. Code 5 is at risk of absorbing what belongs in codes 2 and 3.**
Where displacement arrives as a *personal, addressed threat* — the gang's warning message in
Haiti, the X marked on 30 doors in Caracas — respondents will reach for "other threats of
violence against you". Analytically these are public-order collapse and political persecution.
Sequencing and probing matter.
([OCCRP](https://www.occrp.org/en/news/maduro-opponents-in-venezuela-find-their-houses-marked-with-an-x))

**5. Two gaps the eight options do not cover at all.**
- **Forced return.** Afghanistan's dominant current displacement flow is mass forced return
  from Pakistan and Iran — 2.4 million since 2023 by Pakistan's own count. No response option
  captures being deported back.
- **Cause of non-return, as distinct from cause of departure.** In Iraq, families of alleged
  ISIS affiliates were displaced by conflict (code 1) but their displacement is *sustained*
  by authorities' property confiscation and banishment (code 4). The stem "have you ever left
  a home due to…" misses this entirely.
  ([HRW](https://www.hrw.org/news/2018/04/19/iraq-isis-suspects-homes-confiscated))

## Two boundary problems worth a coding rule

**Codes 4 and 7 collapse where the state clears land for a concession.** Government demolition
of an IDP settlement is simultaneously destruction of property by authorities (4) and eviction
for a project (7). Somalia and Ethiopia produce this event type at scale; so did Mozambique's
2017 expulsion from the ruby fields. Suggested rule: **classify by the actor's stated purpose
— punishment or control → code 4; land clearance for a project → code 7** — and accept
double-coding where both apply.

**Codes 6 and 7 blur in southern Iraq and elsewhere.** Drought is natural; salinisation from
upstream damming, oilfield water use and failed infrastructure is man-made. A respondent from
Basra or the marshes will not distinguish the two, and arguably should not be asked to.
Nigeria's September 2024 Alau Dam collapse — a failed structure causing a flood affecting a
million people — sits in the same ambiguity.

## Where the research found nothing

Stated so the gaps are not mistaken for coverage:

- Development-induced displacement in peacetime Ukraine — none found
- Quantified gold-mining displacement in Sudan — reported, not verified in a mainstream source
- A total figure for Crimean Tatars who left Crimea since 2014 — circulates in press
  reporting, not verifiable against UN or major-NGO sources; **do not cite a number**
- Pollution-driven displacement in the Niger Delta — harm to health and livelihoods
  extensively documented, population displacement not attributed
- Development-induced displacement in South Sudan and CAR — no resettlement programme found
- SOGI-driven displacement in 18 of the 20 countries — abuse documented, flight not

## Method and limitations

Twenty countries researched in parallel via web search and retrieval, prioritising Human
Rights Watch, Amnesty International, OHCHR and UN Commissions of Inquiry, IDMC, NRC,
International Crisis Group, and peer-reviewed or major-outlet reporting. Every claim in the
persecution, state-abuse and man-made sections carries a source URL.

The enumerator examples produced alongside this note are **constructed plain-language
phrasings grounded in the documented events**, not verbatim respondent quotations. They are
intended for cognitive interview stimuli and enumerator training, and should be reviewed by
country teams before field use.

This is qualitative and non-exhaustive. It establishes that codes 3, 4 and 7 describe real
and often large phenomena in these countries. It does **not** establish how many people would
select each option, which is what the survey is for.
